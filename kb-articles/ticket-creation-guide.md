# Creating and Managing Support Tickets: Complete Guide

## Overview

Support tickets are the foundation of efficient IT support. This comprehensive guide walks you through creating, managing, and tracking support tickets with GigitekAI. Whether you're an end-user reporting an issue or a support technician managing requests, understanding the ticket creation process ensures faster resolution and better communication.

## What is a Support Ticket?

A support ticket is a formal record of your support request. It documents the issue you're experiencing, creates a unique tracking number, and allows both you and the support team to communicate about the problem until it's resolved. Each ticket is assigned a priority level, category, and status to ensure it receives appropriate attention.

**Benefits of Using Support Tickets:**
- Creates an official record of your issue
- Provides a unique ticket number for reference
- Enables asynchronous communication (no need to be online simultaneously)
- Allows multiple team members to contribute to the solution
- Tracks issue history and resolution time
- Provides accountability and quality assurance
- Enables reporting and analytics on support performance

## Step-by-Step: Creating Your First Ticket

### Step 1: Access the Ticket Creation Form

Log into the GigitekAI Support Portal and click the **"Create New Ticket"** button. You will see a form with several fields to complete.

### Step 2: Select Your Service Category

Choose the service or product your issue relates to from the dropdown menu. Common categories include:

- **Microsoft 365 / Office 365** - Issues with Outlook, Teams, SharePoint, OneDrive, etc.
- **Cloud Infrastructure** - Azure, cloud storage, or cloud application issues
- **Security & Compliance** - Password resets, MFA, security concerns
- **Helpdesk & Support** - General support requests or account issues
- **Billing & Licensing** - License questions, billing inquiries
- **Other** - Issues that don't fit other categories

Selecting the correct category helps route your ticket to the right team and speeds up resolution.

### Step 3: Choose Your Issue Type

Select the type of issue you're experiencing:

- **Incident** - Something is broken or not working (urgent)
- **Service Request** - You need help with a task or process
- **Change Request** - You want to modify or update something
- **Question** - You have a general question about a service
- **Feedback** - You want to provide suggestions or report a bug

### Step 4: Set the Priority Level

Indicate how urgent your issue is. Priority levels help the support team prioritize their work:

| Priority | Description | Response Time | Example |
|----------|-------------|----------------|---------|
| **Critical** | Business is stopped, multiple users affected | 15 minutes | Email server down, all Teams unavailable |
| **High** | Significant impact, workaround unavailable | 1 hour | Cannot access critical application |
| **Medium** | Moderate impact, some workaround exists | 4 hours | Feature not working, but can use alternative |
| **Low** | Minimal impact, cosmetic issue | 8 hours | UI display issue, general question |

**Important:** Only mark tickets as Critical if business operations are truly stopped. Overusing Critical priority can delay actual emergencies.

### Step 5: Write a Clear Subject Line

The subject line should be concise but descriptive. It's the first thing the support team sees and helps them quickly understand your issue.

**Good Subject Lines:**
- "Cannot send emails in Outlook - Error 0x80040111"
- "OneDrive sync stopped after Windows update"
- "MFA not working on Teams mobile app"
- "Need to reset password for new employee"

**Poor Subject Lines:**
- "Help!"
- "It's broken"
- "Urgent issue"
- "Problem with computer"

### Step 6: Provide a Detailed Description

This is the most important part of your ticket. Provide enough information for the support team to understand and reproduce your issue without having to ask follow-up questions.

**Include the Following Information:**

**What you were trying to do:**
Describe the task or action you were attempting. For example, "I was trying to send a meeting invitation to 50 people through Outlook."

**What happened instead:**
Describe what went wrong. For example, "The invitation failed to send and I received an error message."

**Error messages:**
If you received an error message, copy and paste the exact text. Include any error codes or numbers.

**When it started:**
Indicate when the problem began. For example, "This started happening after I updated Windows yesterday" or "This has been happening for the past week."

**What you've already tried:**
List any troubleshooting steps you've already attempted. This helps the support team avoid suggesting solutions you've already tried.

**Impact:**
Describe how this issue is affecting your work. For example, "I cannot complete my project deadline" or "This affects 5 team members."

**Example of a Good Description:**

> I am unable to access SharePoint Online. When I try to navigate to our company SharePoint site (https://company.sharepoint.com), I receive the error "Access Denied - You do not have permission to access this resource." This started happening this morning at approximately 9:00 AM. I have already tried:
> 1. Clearing my browser cache
> 2. Trying a different browser (Chrome instead of Edge)
> 3. Logging out and back into Office 365
> 
> This is affecting my ability to access project documents that are stored in SharePoint. I need access restored as soon as possible.

### Step 7: Attach Relevant Files

If applicable, attach screenshots, error messages, or documents that help illustrate your issue. To attach files:

1. Click the **"Attach Files"** button
2. Select files from your computer
3. Files will be uploaded to your ticket

**Helpful Attachments:**
- Screenshots showing the error or issue
- Error messages or logs
- Configuration files (if applicable)
- Documents related to your issue

**Do Not Attach:**
- Sensitive personal information (passwords, credit card numbers)
- Proprietary or confidential company data
- Executable files or scripts
- Large files (over 25 MB)

### Step 8: Provide Contact Information

Ensure your contact information is accurate:

- **Email Address** - Where ticket updates will be sent
- **Phone Number** - For urgent follow-up (optional)
- **Preferred Contact Method** - Email, phone, or Teams chat

### Step 9: Review and Submit

Before submitting, review all information for accuracy:

- [ ] Service category is correct
- [ ] Issue type is appropriate
- [ ] Priority level is accurate
- [ ] Subject line is clear and descriptive
- [ ] Description includes all relevant details
- [ ] Contact information is correct
- [ ] Attachments are included (if applicable)

Once you've verified everything, click the **"Submit Ticket"** button. You will receive a confirmation message with your **Ticket Number**. Save this number for your records.

## Understanding Your Ticket Number

Your ticket number is a unique identifier for your support request. It typically follows the format: **GG-XXXXXX** (where X represents numbers).

**Example:** GG-123456

Use your ticket number when:
- Following up on your issue
- Referencing the ticket in emails or calls
- Tracking the status of your request
- Escalating to management

## Ticket Status Workflow

Your ticket will move through several statuses as it's being worked on:

| Status | Meaning | What to Expect |
|--------|---------|----------------|
| **New** | Ticket just created | Support team will review shortly |
| **Open** | Being reviewed or investigated | Support team is working on it |
| **In Progress** | Active troubleshooting underway | Updates will be provided regularly |
| **Waiting for Customer** | Support team needs information from you | Respond with requested information |
| **Waiting for Vendor** | Escalated to third-party vendor | May take longer, you'll be updated |
| **Resolved** | Issue has been fixed | Support team will verify with you |
| **Closed** | Ticket completed and archived | Can be reopened if issue recurs |

## Best Practices for Ticket Management

### For End Users

**When Creating Tickets:**
- Be specific and detailed in your description
- Include error messages and screenshots
- Mention what you've already tried
- Set appropriate priority levels
- Provide accurate contact information

**While Your Ticket is Open:**
- Respond promptly to support team requests
- Provide additional information if asked
- Test solutions suggested by support
- Keep your ticket focused on one issue (create separate tickets for different issues)
- Avoid duplicate tickets for the same issue

**After Resolution:**
- Verify the solution works for you
- Provide feedback on your support experience
- Rate your satisfaction with the resolution
- Close the ticket if you're satisfied
- Reopen if the issue recurs

### For Support Technicians

**When Receiving Tickets:**
- Acknowledge receipt within SLA timeframe
- Categorize and prioritize appropriately
- Assign to correct team member
- Review ticket history for context

**While Investigating:**
- Provide regular status updates
- Ask clarifying questions if needed
- Document all troubleshooting steps
- Test solutions before providing them
- Escalate if necessary

**When Resolving:**
- Verify the solution with the customer
- Provide clear documentation of what was done
- Suggest preventive measures
- Update knowledge base if applicable
- Close ticket only after customer confirmation

## Common Ticket Scenarios

### Scenario 1: Cannot Access Microsoft 365

**Ticket Details:**
- Category: Microsoft 365
- Type: Incident
- Priority: High
- Subject: "Cannot access Office 365 applications"
- Description: Include which applications you cannot access (Outlook, Teams, etc.), when it started, and any error messages

**Expected Resolution Time:** 1-4 hours

### Scenario 2: Password Reset Request

**Ticket Details:**
- Category: Security & Compliance
- Type: Service Request
- Priority: Medium
- Subject: "Password reset request for [your name]"
- Description: Confirm your identity and specify which account needs the reset

**Expected Resolution Time:** 15 minutes to 1 hour

### Scenario 3: New User Setup

**Ticket Details:**
- Category: Helpdesk & Support
- Type: Service Request
- Priority: Medium
- Subject: "New user setup for [employee name]"
- Description: Include start date, department, and required applications

**Expected Resolution Time:** 2-4 hours

### Scenario 4: Software Installation

**Ticket Details:**
- Category: Helpdesk & Support
- Type: Service Request
- Priority: Low
- Subject: "Request to install [software name]"
- Description: Explain why you need the software and how many licenses are required

**Expected Resolution Time:** 1-2 business days

## Escalation Process

If your issue is not resolved within the expected timeframe or requires higher-level attention:

### Step 1: Contact the Support Team

Email or call the support team and reference your ticket number. Explain why you believe escalation is necessary.

### Step 2: Escalation Review

The support manager will review your ticket and determine if escalation is warranted.

### Step 3: Escalated Investigation

Your ticket will be assigned to a senior technician or specialist for further investigation.

### Step 4: Resolution or Further Action

The escalated team will either resolve the issue or take additional steps (vendor escalation, management review, etc.).

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: How long does it take to resolve a ticket?**
A: Resolution time depends on priority level and complexity. See the SLA table above for typical timeframes.

**Q: Can I create a ticket by phone?**
A: You can call support and they can create a ticket for you, but the web portal is the preferred method.

**Q: What if I need to add information to my ticket after submitting it?**
A: Reply to any support email or add a comment through the ticket portal. Your response will be added to the ticket.

**Q: Can I have someone else manage my ticket?**
A: You can authorize another person to manage your ticket by adding them as a watcher or delegating access through the portal.

**Q: What happens if I don't respond to support requests?**
A: If a ticket is waiting for your response for more than 7 days, it may be automatically closed. You can reopen it by contacting support.

**Q: Can I delete a ticket?**
A: No, tickets cannot be deleted for compliance and audit purposes. They can be closed or archived.

**Q: How long are tickets kept in the system?**
A: Tickets are retained for at least 2 years for compliance and reference purposes.
